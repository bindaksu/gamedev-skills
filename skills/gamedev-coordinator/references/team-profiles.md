# How Top-Grossing Studios Structure Teams

Read when sizing a team, explaining why a role exists, or choosing between a shared-platform model and fully autonomous game teams.

**Method note.** Compiled from public sources on 2026-10-08. LinkedIn and other people-profile data were **not** scraped or used. Job postings, careers pages, interviews and press were used as a proxy for "what team members do". Tags carried from the research: [proxy] = inferred from postings/press; [secondary] = third party relaying another's data; [estimate] = headcount estimator; [inference] = synthesis, not sourced; [unverified] = from a search summary. Every headcount carries its year; most org evidence is 2019–2024, revenue is 2025. Treat all of it as (as of 2026-10; verify).

Supercell formalized the split as "old Supercell" (new games, small teams) and "new Supercell" (live games as business units) (https://mobilegamer.biz/supercell-has-now-been-effectively-split-in-two-says-boss-paananen/).

## 2. Shared platform plus autonomous game teams

Most large operators separate the creative game team from a central live-ops, data and UA platform:

- **Scopely Playgami**: internal SaaS for game operations under every title; internal and partner studios run "as a single team" on it (https://scopely.com/about/ ; https://naavik.co/digest/dissecting-scopelys-mobile-empire/).
- **Playtika Boost**: live ops, CRM, marketing, AI/ML, automation; acquired studios keep creative autonomy (https://naavik.co/digest/playtika-hitting-the-slots-with-superplay/).
- **Playrix**: building a unified cross-title LiveOps Platform for A/B tests, events, balance and content delivery (https://hitmarker.net/jobs/playrix-python-software-engineer-liveops-platform-1650314 [proxy]).
- **Supercell**: MAKE platform and central Social/AI tech (https://supercell.com/en/news/best-days/7600/); in-house Titan engine run by a ~70-person org (https://supercell.com/en/news/game-engine-called-titan).
- **King**: in-house engine and Shared Tech, "the engine that powers all our live games" (https://hitmarker.net/jobs/king-senior-backend-engineer-1745379 [proxy]).
- **Tencent Level Infinite**: a global publishing layer distinct from development (https://www.pocketgamer.biz/tencent-launches-new-publishing-division-level-infinite).

Rule of thumb: game teams own creative work; the platform owns experimentation, event tooling, CRM and UA.

## 3. Leadership: GM / franchise owner, not a director stack

King runs franchise GMs and treats "the entire game" as live operations (https://mobilegamer.biz/candy-crush-boss-todd-green-on-live-ops-excellence-new-games-and-that-microsoft-takeover/). Supercell cells decide what to build and whether to kill it while management "facilitates" (https://supercell.com/en/news/level-two-freedom-and-responsibility/7623/). Lilith makes each game a standalone subsidiary with 20% core-team equity (https://pocketgamer.biz/inside-mobile-hitmaker-lilith-games/). Live teams add leads, producers and people partners as they pass ~40 people (Brawl Stars, above).

## 4. Builders vs federated operators

- **Builders** grow organically and stay lean: Supercell, Dream Games, miHoYo, Peak.
- **Federated M&A operators** buy proven teams, keep founders, bolt on the platform: Scopely, Playtika, Zynga, Moon Active, Century Games.

## 5. Kill rates and soft-launch durations

- Supercell: 30+ games killed against 5 hits in 12 years (2023) (https://mobidictum.com/supercell-ceo-ilkka-paananen-talks-about-nfts-and-why-they-kill-so-many-games/); Clash Mini killed after 2+ years of soft launch (https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/03/14/all-games-killed-by-supercell-everdale-hay-day-pop-clash-mini); Squad Busters was its first globally launched game closed, staff redeployed (https://mobilegamer.biz/supercell-is-killing-squad-busters/).
- Dream Games: Royal Kingdom spent **19 months** in soft launch (https://www.pocketgamer.biz/dream-games-launches-royal-kingdom-worldwide-as-royal-match-nears-4-billion).
- Scopely: Monopoly GO took ~6 years concept-to-market with a full genre pivot from midcore PvP (https://www.pocketgamer.biz/monopoly-go-celebrates-its-first-birthday-and-shares-its-behind-the-scenes-story/).
- Lilith: soft launches in small, culturally similar markets, gates on retention, reluctant to kill on short-term misses (https://pocketgamer.biz/inside-mobile-hitmaker-lilith-games/), yet cut staff after Project Party failed in soft launch (https://wnhub.io/news/hr/item-46191).
- Habby: long soft launches that themselves earn revenue; Archero 2 made $65.3m including soft launch (https://www.pocketgamer.biz/archero-2-makes-328m-in-first-30-days-from-player-spending).

## 6. Estimated discipline mix of a live team

**No sourced discipline ratio exists in public material.** The mix below is [inference] from postings and the datapoints above:

| Discipline | Share |
|---|---|
| Engineering (client, server, tools) | 30–40% |
| Art and tech art | 25–35% |
| Design (game, level, economy, live ops) | 15–20% |
| Product, data, QA, community | 10–15% |

UA and creative production usually sit centrally. Roles that recur in live-team postings: live-ops/event designers, a PM or GM owning KPIs and the event/offer calendar, data analysts running A/B tests, community managers, live-ops QA, economy designers, client/backend/tools engineers, a large art function, marketing creative and UA. Dream Games and Peak hire generalist "Product Specialist / PM, Games" (design + data + A/B) where King, Playrix, Scopely and Supercell split level, economy and LiveOps into specialist titles.

## 8. Cross-cutting lessons

1. Revenue per head varies 5x or more; Dream Games (~300 people, ~$5m/head on mixed bases) vs Playtika/Moon Active (~$0.8–0.9m/head). Headcount is not a moat [inference].
2. Kill early and cheaply, then soft launch for a long time.
3. Iterate on a proven formula: Kingshot from Whiteout (https://naavik.co/digest/century-games-4x-portfolio-strategy/), Royal Match from Peak's Toon Blast lineage.
4. Live ops becomes the product: Candy Crush adds levels weekly (https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/inside-live-ops-at-king-how-candy-crush-stays-fresh-after-14-years); Genshin ships roughly 6-week patches [fan source].
5. AI is restructuring content roles in 2025: King cut level design, UX and copy roles (anonymous sourcing, not on-record) (https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/laid-off-king-staff-are-reportedly-being-replaced-by-ai-tools-they-helped-build); Supercell is hiring LLM/agent engineers (https://supercell.com/en/careers/principal-llm-engineer/2121055).
6. Monetization is moving off-store: FunPlus webshops exceed 25% of mobile revenue (https://funplus.com/info/chris-petrovic-predictions-for-2026/).

## Gaps (do not infer)

No public headcount for Tencent's HoK team, NetEase, FirstFun or Century's Whiteout team; no engine confirmed for Whiteout, Last War or Lilith titles; no sourced engineer-to-art-to-design ratio for any studio.
